## Page-by-page findings

### Printed source p117 — Forklift cargo loading/unloading basic operation
**Status: MISSING / NOT VERIFIED in the corresponding Study Companion page section.**
The original source page contains a distinct eight-step sequence:
1. Reduce speed when approaching cargo.
2. Stop immediately in front of the cargo and bring the mast upright.
3. Check that the cargo is not likely to collapse and that there are no hazards.
4. Align the forks straight with the cargo; identify the fork-entry openings and insert slowly. Avoid striking the pallet and do not push it out from the opposite side by inserting too deeply.
5. Initially raise only 5–10 cm, draw the cargo 10–20 cm towards the forklift and lower it; then insert the forks fully until the cargo slightly contacts the fork heel/backrest before lifting.
6. Reverse slowly to a safe lowering area; lower the cargo base to 5–10 cm, tilt the mast sufficiently backwards, and confirm stability.
7. To place cargo, return the mast upright, raise slightly above the placement position, move forward slowly and lower the pallet; withdraw the forks 10–20 cm, raise again, push into the designated position and lower.
8. When retrieving a pallet from a high position, check for people nearby and surrounding cargo.
The DOCX section headed “SOURCE PAGE 117” instead begins with truck-driver precautions that belong to printed source p118. The eight-step p117 procedure is not present in that SOURCE PAGE 117 section. A separate generic eight-step sequence mentioned in an earlier batch must not be treated as proof that this page-specific procedure is covered.

**Required correction:** Add the complete p117 sequence with all measurements, order, warnings and English/Sinhala explanations. Map it to printed source p117.

### Printed source p118 — Loading/unloading precautions and stacking
**Status: PARTIALLY COVERED; page mapping defect.**
The truck-driver precautions are present in the DOCX under SOURCE PAGE 117:
- communicate with the truck driver and verify safety shoes and helmet;
- use equipment suitable for cargo shape and weight;
- confirm stability after inserting forks or gripping;
- use an additional forklift or another safe method for long cargo;
- keep checking the driver's location during loading and ensure an escape location during unloading.
These are the source p118 items, so their current placement under SOURCE PAGE 117 is incorrect.

The stacking content is present under SOURCE PAGE 118:
- understand packaging strength and stack to a suitable height;
- use portable racks/nestainers or pallet supports when cartons or contents cannot bear the load;
- check floor load capacity;
- use correct fork insertion depth and restack unstable cargo;
- inspect the completed stack and correct instability;
- do not stack empty pallets to 30 tiers or more.
These items substantially match source p118.

**Required correction:** Move/relabel the truck-driver precautions to printed p118 (or include them within the p118 section), while retaining the stacking content and its p118 mapping. Ensure there is no duplicated or lost content during the correction.

### Printed source p119 — Forklift parking
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes the designated parking area rule, prohibition on obstructing other machines/vehicles, all five parking principles in order (lower forks, apply parking brake, stop engine, remove key, apply wheel chock), and the requirement to perform all five before leaving the operator seat and inform the supervisor of destination and expected absence.

### Printed source p120 — Pallet cargo operating procedure
**Status: SUBSTANTIALLY COVERED.**
All eight source points are represented:
- choose forklift and fork length to suit weight/dimensions;
- set fork width and approach from the front;
- insert deeply enough to support the whole pallet;
- check ground clearance, centre of gravity and stability after lifting;
- reverse when forward visibility is blocked, including the source's reach-type forklift rule;
- watch uneven floors, level changes, slopes and obstacles; reduce speed and cross slopes straight on;
- obey the warehouse speed limit;
- stop and adjust fork angle before taking a pallet from a slope.
The DOCX also contains a source-illustration placeholder/table for p120. The actual illustration-to-source visual match remains for Step 3.

### Printed source p121 — FIBC and roll cargo
**Status: SUBSTANTIALLY COVERED.**
FIBC section includes dedicated lifting attachment, the alternative secured jig/sling method, prohibition on hanging from one fork, pallet transport as the general rule, overturning risk from swinging bags, and avoiding sudden starts/stops/turns.
Roll cargo section includes matching clamp forklift to weight/width/diameter, limitation to container/truck loading and unloading rather than long-distance travel, perpendicular clamp orientation, gripping the centre, checking two-roll diameter differences and secure grip, pallet-cargo travel precautions, and adjusting clamp pressure to cargo shape.
Step 3 still needs visual verification of the source illustrations and equipment diagrams.

### Printed source p122 — Container work
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes:
- vanning with weight balance and stability, plus lashing/shoring when needed;
- devanning with slow, one-at-a-time door opening while checking for collapsed cargo;
- securing doors against unexpected movement;
- immediate reporting of cargo-collapse risk;
- switching off power before moving a belt conveyor;
- appointing a work leader when connecting two or more conveyors;
- providing an emergency-stop device/button on belt conveyors.
The DOCX also includes an end-of-source note about the cooperating Japanese associations and July 2026 publication date; this is supplementary publication information.
