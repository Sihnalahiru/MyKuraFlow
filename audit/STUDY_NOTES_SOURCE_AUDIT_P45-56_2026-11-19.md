## Page-by-page findings

### Printed p45 — Inventory quantity control
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX contains inventory definition, regular quantity checking, stock/cargo tag purpose, updating tags when cargo enters or leaves, separate counting by receiving/LOT number, visible tag placement, orderly storage locations, and the full three-item exception table:
- tag quantity differs from actual quantity;
- cargo is broken or dirty;
- an unusual/abnormal condition occurs.
It also directs immediate reporting to the work-management responsible person.

### Printed p46 — Stocktaking and sample release
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes the purpose of stocktaking, comparison of book/data quantity with actual quantity, cargo/packaging condition checks, all three types (periodic, full/whole-site and cycle counting), the three required record fields (responsible person's name, date and counted quantity), and immediate reporting/investigation of inventory discrepancies.
Sample release coverage includes the release instruction, identity/depositor check, business-hours limit and protection of other depositors' cargo.

### Printed p47 — Sample-release controls and disposal/destruction
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes employee attendance during the depositor's work, stopping unauthorised removal, promptly escorting the depositor out and to the office, separate storage and warning tag for sampled cargo, fixed storage location, cargo-ledger record and a system for later staff to identify sampled cargo. It distinguishes disposal (廃棄) from destruction (滅却), defines industrial waste with source examples, and prohibits personal/secret disposal without supervisor instruction.

### Printed p48 — Rain leakage, rodents and warehouse closing
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes inspection inside and outside during rain, ceiling/wall leakage, floor water, gutters/drains, prompt reporting and repair; rodent damage, trapping equipment and sealing entry gaps; and end-of-day closing steps. The three pre-lock checks (no person left inside, no fire risk or water leakage, and important/hazardous items properly stored) are also included.

### Printed p49 — Fire prevention, forklift charging and water-disaster preparedness
**Status: PARTIAL — water-disaster section missing.**
The DOCX covers the prohibition on using fire inside the warehouse; hydrogen and oxygen gas during forklift battery charging and ignition/explosion risk; keeping access clear to fire extinguishers and hydrants; regular inspection of fire equipment; appointment of a fire-prevention manager; and the self-defence firefighting organisation with the source's applicable building/facility examples.

However, the original printed p49 also contains a distinct water-disaster section that is not present in the DOCX SOURCE PAGE 49 section:
- river overflow caused by heavy rain;
- storm surge associated with typhoons and tsunami associated with earthquakes;
- keep sandbags ready to block incoming water;
- know the warehouse's water-disaster response procedure in advance;
- check the local hazard map to understand risks from nearby rivers and surrounding areas.

**Required correction:** Add the complete water-disaster preparedness section under printed p49, with English and Sinhala explanations. Preserve the distinction between river flooding, storm surge and tsunami.

### Printed p50 — Emergency response: earthquake, fire and power failure
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes prior knowledge of exits and evacuation routes, keeping exits/routes clear, not using elevators during evacuation and following green exit arrows. It covers all four earthquake response steps, the elevator-specific response and trapped-passenger buzzer/intercom actions, all three fire response steps, the three fire-extinguisher operation steps, and the three refrigerated-warehouse power-failure steps (portable/forklift light, move to exit, manually open door and leave).

### Printed p51 — Pallet definition and common sizes
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX defines pallets and forklift entry, and reproduces the four source sizes and uses:
- 11-type: 1,100 × 1,100 mm, most common standard pallet;
- 12-type: 1,200 × 1,000 mm, commonly used in refrigerated warehouses;
- 14-type: 1,400 × 1,100 mm, large cargo and larger surface;
- 9-type: 1,100 × 900 mm, beverage pallet / P-pallet.
It also covers T11, JIS, the source's Through Transit explanation and the 144–150 mm standard height range.

### Printed p52 — Pallet materials, capacity, faces and fork-entry direction
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX covers wood, plastic, steel/iron, paper and corrugated-cardboard pallet materials; the source's standard 1-tonne maximum loading mass; single-face and double-face pallets; and two-way/four-way fork-entry distinctions. It warns that pallets vary in strength and that material, condition and rated capacity must be considered.

### Printed p53 — Four-way entry and maximum loading warning
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX explains that a four-way entry pallet accepts forks from all four directions and is easier to approach from any direction. It includes the explicit 1-tonne maximum-load warning and the risk of pallet breakage and accidents if the limit is exceeded.

### Printed p54 — Work clothing and PPE
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX covers the basic workwear set (work shirt, jacket and work trousers), fan-equipped clothing used to help prevent heatstroke, reflective safety vest, mandatory helmet inside the warehouse, protective safety shoes and gloves. It includes the hygiene-related possibility of separate gloves for distribution-processing work.

### Printed p55 — Receiving documents
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX defines forms/documents (帳票) and includes all four receiving documents with purposes and alternative company names: deposit application, receiving instruction slip, cargo receipt and receiving notification. It preserves the agreement to warehouse terms and the cargo details recorded on the application.

### Printed p56 — Dispatch documents, labels and identification codes
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes the picking-list definition, all four dispatch documents (dispatch slip, dispatch instruction, delivery/consignment document and dispatch notification), dispatch and receiving labels, management/LOT numbers, JAN barcode and the source's stated 12-digit description, GTIN terminology and QR's Quick Response expansion and two-dimensional barcode distinction.
