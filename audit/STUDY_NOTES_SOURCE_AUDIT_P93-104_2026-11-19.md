## Scope and method
Compared the original Japanese source textbook printed pages 93–104 with the matching SOURCE PAGE sections and bilingual text in the Master DOCX. Source PDF text was extracted page-by-page and compared with the DOCX paragraphs and tables. A page heading, a generic reminder to consult the source, or an image placeholder is not treated as full coverage.

## Bulk findings

### Page 93 – Vaccination, health checks and heatstroke prevention
**MAJOR GAP – most heatstroke prevention content is missing.**
Present:
- Preventive vaccination for infectious diseases such as COVID-19 and influenza.
- Attend company-directed health examination at the specified date, time and place.
- General definition of heatstroke as impaired body-temperature regulation after prolonged exposure to heat.
Missing:
- Warehouse indoor temperature can become very high in summer.
- Do not remove work clothes or helmet while working.
- Drink water frequently in small amounts even when not thirsty.
- Warning symptoms during work: heavy sweating and feeling unwell.
- If symptoms appear, stop work immediately and provide first aid.
Add these source instructions in English and Sinhala. Preserve the mandatory helmet/work-clothing warning.

### Page 94 – Heatstroke first aid
**MAJOR GAP – the three first-aid steps are absent.**
Present: speak to a nearby person who may have heatstroke, provide the same response, and seek hospital/medical attention immediately if first aid does not improve the condition.
Missing:
1. Move the person to a cool room with air conditioning.
2. Loosen clothing and cool the body.
3. Give water and salt/electrolytes (source example: sports drink) and let the person rest.
Also missing the page's summary checklist: do not overwork when unwell; perform morning warm-up; receive preventive vaccination; drink fluids frequently; stop work immediately if heatstroke is suspected.
Add as a numbered bilingual first-aid table and summary checklist. Keep the source sequence intact.

### Page 95 – PPE and basic safe-work rules
**PARTIAL – gloves and fundamental work principles missing.**
Present: wear specified workwear; helmet must be worn whenever entering warehouse, chin strap fastened; safety shoes required and protect toes.
Missing:
- Gloves must be worn; do not touch cargo or machinery with bare hands.
- For distribution-processing food work, use vinyl/plastic gloves for hygiene.
- Follow established work rules at all times; safety and accuracy take priority; never break rules even when in a hurry.
Add the missing rules in English and Sinhala.

### Page 96 – Manual handling rules and correct lifting
**MAJOR GAP – multiple high-priority safety rules are missing.**
Present:
- Use level, straight and short route.
- Avoid repeated lifting/moving/setting down.
- Reduce lifting/lowering time.
- Do not work above head height.
- Do not middle-pull cargo from stacked piles.
- Do not work facing backwards; do not jump over obstacles or climb stacked cargo.
Missing:
- Do not swing long objects around.
- Avoid manually carrying objects that roll easily or are long.
- Follow all precautions when handling dangerous or harmful substances.
- Cargo heavier than 40% of a worker's body weight (24% for women, as stated by this source) must be carried by two or more people.
- Keep the time cargo is held by hand as short as possible.
- Correct lifting method: bend knees and move close to cargo; hold cargo close to the body around waist level; lift by slowly straightening knees. Keep source wording and values exact; do not add external ergonomic thresholds.
Add a complete bilingual rule list and three-step lifting sequence.

### Page 97 – Cage trolley handling and warehouse hazard points
**MAJOR GAP – most operating controls are missing.**
Present:
- Cage trolley / roll-box pallet definition.
- One-person handling limit: less than 300 kg.
- Check caster stopper is free before moving.
- Hold vertical frame between chest and waist height and push forward.
- When extracting from truck bed, move backwards while pulling to a position where it can be pushed forward.
Missing:
- Check forward route; call out where visibility is poor.
- Slow down from 2 metres before stopping or turning.
- During transport, secure near centre of gravity with lashing belt and fix firmly using shoring bar.
- Support the shoring bar when removing it so it does not fall.
- If cargo is stacked high and blocks forward view, pull the cage trolley backwards.
- Lock the stopper after use.
- Heavy cargo: use a trolley; avoid carrying by hand while holding it.
- Foot-level hazards: do not stack so high that forward view is blocked; use trolley and watch the walking path.
Add the missing bilingual controls, preserving the 300 kg and 2 m values.

### Page 98 – Working at height and machinery
**SUBSTANTIALLY COVERED; one machinery-contact warning needs explicit inclusion.**
Present:
- Prohibited climbing onto shelves/pillars.
- Never ride on pallet lifted by forklift.
- Do not stand on top platform of stepladder.
- Do not use ladder on unstable ground.
- Safe work platform, purpose-built elevated work vehicle, or lower pallet to floor before work.
Missing/not explicit:
- Do not lean on a belt conveyor.
- Moving belt can catch hands/clothing and cause serious injury.
- Remove cargo while maintaining a safe posture and never touching the belt.
Add the conveyor-specific warning to page 98 if it is not already present elsewhere with an explicit cross-reference.

### Page 99 – Safety signs and passageway rules
**PARTIAL – detailed passageway rules are deferred to the next page.**
Present:
- Signs/notices must be understood and followed.
- Tiger tape: diagonal stripes, commonly yellow/black; around pillars, forklift operating areas and door-opening areas; indicates danger; also called warning tape/police line.
- General instruction to follow safe passageway signs and keep routes clear.
Missing from the page 99 section:
- Always walk on designated safety walkways inside the warehouse.
- Do not leave objects in passageways; put items away after use because they can cause trips/collisions.
- Keep passageways sufficiently illuminated using daylight or lighting.
Add these rules in English and Sinhala rather than merely referring to page 100.

### Page 100 – Indoor passageways and forklift proximity
**MAJOR GAP – most numerical and operating rules are missing.**
Present: definition of floor breakthrough (踏み抜き).
Missing:
- Indoor passageway must have sufficient width for its use.
- Floor must be free of tripping, slipping and floor-breakthrough hazards.
- No obstacles within 1.8 metres above floor level.
- Passage between machines or between machine and other equipment must be at least 80 cm wide.
- Do not enter restricted areas; even without a sign, do not enter areas unrelated to one's work.
- Do not pass close to forklifts; stop work and confirm safety when forklift approaches.
- Before passing behind forklift, call out to driver.
- Before crossing a forklift route, use pointing-and-calling (指差し呼称) and confirm safety.
Add the complete bilingual table, retaining 1.8 m and 80 cm exactly.

No material omission found in this pass.

### Page 102 – KYT and 4-round hazard prediction
**MAJOR GAP – purpose and first three rounds are missing.**
Present:
- 4th round: select priority action items and all workers point-and-call the action goal.
- Pointing-and-calling method: look, point, speak clearly; example “Right, left, ahead – clear.”
- Source's experiment conclusion that errors fall to about one-sixth compared with no checking.
Missing:
- Definition of KYT: Kiken (危険 = danger), Yochi (予知 = prediction), Training (訓練).
- Purpose: use work-scene illustrations to find hidden dangers and consider how to work safely; develop hazard-awareness ability.
- Importance of imagination; reject fixed assumptions such as “we always do it this way” or “nothing can be done”; ask “what if it is dangerous?”
- 1R: identify hidden dangers and share possible accidents.
- 2R: select the most dangerous point.
- 3R: propose concrete countermeasures.
Add all four rounds in order with bilingual explanations.

### Page 103 – 5S overview, Seiri and Seiton
**COVERED at text level.**
Present:
- 5S is formed from Seiri, Seiton, Seiso, Seiketsu and Shitsuke.
- Seiri: separate necessary and unnecessary items and discard unnecessary items; avoid space/work waste.
- Seiton: return items to designated places, make them easy to retrieve and group conveniently.
No material omission found in this pass.

### Page 104 – Seiso, Seiketsu and Shitsuke
**COVERED at text level.**
Present:
- Seiso: clean workplace; protect goods and people; remove rubbish.
- Seiketsu: maintain a clean and hygienic condition.
- Shitsuke: develop correct language, manners and behaviour as habits; follow warehouse rules and use appropriate Japanese.
No material omission found in this pass.
