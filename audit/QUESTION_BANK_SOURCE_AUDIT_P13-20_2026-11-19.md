## Source facts not adequately represented in the current page 13–20 question set
The following source details need dedicated, non-duplicative question coverage. These are gaps in the reviewed question set, not invented syllabus additions.

### Printed page 13 — warehouse floor and loading area
- Floor load limit is expressed per 1 m² or per 1 tsubo; 1 tsubo is approximately 3.3 m².
- Exceeding the floor load limit can damage the floor and is dangerous; workers must check the limit.
- Dust-proof coating makes cleaning easier, reduces dust contamination of goods, and helps maintain hygiene.
- Platform / truck bay (バース) is the place where trucks stop for loading and unloading.

### Printed page 14 — building and wall equipment
- Dock leveller removes the height difference between the warehouse platform and truck/container floor to support smooth loading and unloading.
- Nizuri (荷摺り) is a thick board/bar fitted to the inside wall to protect the wall if cargo collapses against it.
- Shutters are used at external entrances and as fire shutters inside the warehouse.
- Fire shutters are legally required at prescribed area boundaries.
- Dock shelters close the gap around each truck/container body to keep out wind, rain, and insects.

### Printed page 15 — ceiling and fire equipment
- A beam is a horizontal structural member supporting the ceiling together with columns; warehouse clear height is measured from the underside of the beam to the floor.
- Suspended ceilings are hung using metal bolts/framework and may be used for offices inside warehouses.
- A mezzanine is an assembled intermediate storage level that increases usable storage space.
- Fire extinguishers are first-response equipment for putting out small fires.
- Fire hydrants contain hoses and deliver water to extinguish fires.
- Sprinklers detect heat/smoke and automatically discharge water; the source says large and automated warehouses are legally required to install them.
- Fire alarm equipment detects heat/smoke and sounds an emergency bell; workers must immediately evacuate to a safe place.

### Printed page 16 — racks and storage equipment
- Fixed racks are floor-fixed; source lists space utilization, easier handling, and a possible fourth storage level when a three-level rack has sufficient ceiling clearance.
- Mobile racks move on the floor and can be brought together to use horizontal space efficiently.
- Nestainers are stackable/compact when not in use and assembled when needed; storage can be adjusted to cargo volume.
- 正ネス: load-supporting grid is at the lower level; cargo can remain loaded while the unit is moved/stacked; suitable where layouts change; a pallet top can allow further stacking.
- 逆ネス: grid is at the upper level; one unit can provide two levels; first-level pallet rests directly on the ground; hand lifts can handle pallet in/out; the nestainer itself cannot be moved as a whole.
- Pallet supporters are fitted on racks to support pallets, prevent falling, accommodate size mismatch, and distribute load to reduce rack stress.

### Printed page 17 — automated and auxiliary warehouse equipment
- Automated warehouse systems use a stacker crane to move cargo automatically; source lists faster work and reduced manual labour.
- Surveillance cameras support theft prevention, checking correct work, and identifying waste/inefficiency.
- Illuminated guidance signs help people locate exits during disasters or power failures; the green EXIT sign is the identifying cue.
- Mouse guards prevent mice entering the warehouse.
- Traps / insect-killing lights are installed where insects enter to catch them.

### Printed page 18 — seismic protection
- Seismic-isolation devices are installed below the building and use reinforced rubber to support it.
- They reduce building shaking during earthquakes and help prevent stored goods falling from racks.
- Existing question MKF-WR-0043 covers only the general effect; add coverage for the reinforced-rubber/support mechanism if not covered elsewhere.

### Printed page 19 — cargo elevator and vertical conveyor
- Warehouse freight elevators are for cargo transport.
- The source says no qualification is required merely to operate the elevator, but only cargo handlers and the designated elevator operator may ride; other people may not.
- Maximum payload means the maximum cargo weight permitted; it must never be exceeded.
- Vertical automatic conveyors transport cargo between floors; people must never ride them.
- No special qualification is required to operate the vertical conveyor; loading cargo with a forklift starts it automatically.

### Printed page 20 — forklifts and manual handling equipment
- Counterbalance forklifts are operated seated and suit heavy cargo.
- Reach forklifts are operated standing and are easier to manoeuvre in narrow spaces.
- Forklift driving requires a qualification and prescribed training.
- Counterbalance forklifts usually use two forks to carry pallets; attachments can be changed to suit cargo shape.
- Picking forklifts / order pickers raise the operator platform to access high racks; the source includes different training requirements by cargo weight and working height.
- Hand lifts / hand pallet trucks lift pallets using forks and are moved by human power; no qualification is required.
- Carts are loaded and pushed or pulled by hand.
