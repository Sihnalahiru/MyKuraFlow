## Bulk findings

### Page 81 – Cushioning materials, unpacking and cutter safety
**PARTIAL – cushioning material table and broken-blade procedure missing.**
Present: generic definition of cushioning material; unpacking/master-carton definition and caution not to cut deeply into the goods.
Missing:
- Full five-item cushioning-material table: paper cushioning material; paper packing/filler; air cushion; air-cap/bubble wrap (プチプチ); lightweight foam/polystyrene cushioning.
- If cutter blade breaks: immediately inform the responsible person/nearby workers; search until the broken blade is found; do not leave a broken blade because it is a safety hazard.
The Master DOCX section contains only the table heading and does not reproduce the five rows or the broken-blade procedure.

### Page 82 – PP band cutting, strapping, stretch wrapping and wooden crates
**MAJOR GAP – multiple operations missing.**
Present: automatic strapping machine function and head-clearance warning; general binding-machine definition and hand-clearance warning.
Missing:
- Band cutting (バンド切り): cut PP band binding a set of products and separate them into individual cartons; PP band is a thin plastic strap used to bind cargo.
- Automatic strapping machine operating description as PP band applied rapidly around cargo; retain machine-arm entanglement warning.
- Stretch wrapping (ストレッチ梱包): wrap palletized cargo with stretch film; palletizing means stacking goods on a pallet; stretch film is transparent and elastic; start by trapping film at pallet bottom then wrap bottom-to-top.
- Wooden case / wooden-frame packing: assemble wooden case/frame around cargo.
The notes do not demonstrate these operations as a complete bilingual page section.

### Pages 87–89 – Care-mark appendix
**MAJOR GAP – source contains 11 marks; DOCX covers only a subset.**
Present in notes: handle-with-care, fragile, keep dry, this-way-up/do-not-lay-sideways, do-not-step-on, cutter caution, protect from direct sunlight/heat, keep away from fire, pallet pattern.
Missing/not fully demonstrated:
- Page 88: maximum stacking-layer limit (積段数制限), including that the printed number is the maximum permitted number of tiers (e.g. 5 means maximum 5 layers).
- Page 88: no hand hooks (手かぎ禁止), meaning cargo hook tools must not be used on the carton.
- Page 89: pallet-pattern symbol is not a caution mark; it indicates carton arrangement on a pallet. The existing DOCX wording incorrectly suggests the pallet pattern mark is a caution/handling instruction. Correct this meaning.
- Care-mark appendix needs a full numbered bilingual 1–11 table mapped across pages 87–89; do not rely on selected explanations or image placeholders.
The source list:
1. 取扱注意 – handle with care.
2. 壊れもの – fragile.
3. 水濡れ防止 – keep dry / protect from water.
4. 横積禁止 – this way up / do not lay on side.
5. 踏みつけ厳禁 – do not step on.
6. カッター注意 – cutter caution.
7. 積段数制限 – stacking-layer limit.
8. 手かぎ禁止 – no cargo hooks.
9. 直射日光・熱遮へい – protect from direct sunlight and heat.
10. 火気厳禁 – keep away from fire.
11. パレットパターン – pallet stacking pattern (not a caution mark).

### Page 90 – Working hours and statutory rest
**MAJOR GAP – working-hour limits and daily schedule form missing.**
Present: minimum rest-period rules (more than 6 hours: at least 45 minutes; more than 8 hours: at least 1 hour); “within” definition; note that work hours vary by company.
Missing:
- Textbook limits: no more than 8 hours per day and 40 hours per week.
- Reminder that breaks must be taken even when busy.
- The company work-time record fields: start/end time; morning/lunch/afternoon break times; total break duration; actual daily working time; working days and holidays.
- Morning and end-of-day meetings count as working time; commuting time does not.
- Source warning: workers cannot leave company premises during a break without permission.
Add the complete bilingual rules/table. Since this is legal information, preserve the source's wording and note that the textbook is the source; do not add legal interpretation.

### Page 91 – Occupational accidents and warehouse accident ranking
**MAJOR GAP – numerical accident table and examples absent.**
Present: definition of occupational accident/injury (労働災害 / 労災); general explanation that warehouses use machinery but still involve manual work and risks.
Missing:
- Source ranking based on 2024 confirmed figures (909 total cases), top five:
  1. Falls/slips (転倒): 233 people (26%).
  2. Reaction to movement / awkward or excessive movement (動作の反動・無理な動作): 222 (24%).
  3. Falls from height (墜落・転落): 109 (12%).
  4. Caught-in / entanglement (はさまれ・巻き込まれ): 78 (9%).
  5. Struck by objects (激突され): 70 (8%).
- Common warehouse examples: falling from truck bed/cargo stack; injuries while using cage trolley; caught in belt conveyor/sorter; back injury from heavy cargo or awkward posture.
- Back pain is a common issue; use correct lifting posture.
Add bilingual table and examples; preserve year and figures exactly as source.

### Page 92 – Daily health management and injury prevention
**PARTIAL – mandatory response sequence and warm-up guidance missing.**
Present: climate/environment differences; regular food/fluid intake and daily routine; do not force work when feverish/unwell; general instruction to notify workplace.
Missing:
- Exact sequence when feverish/unwell before work: 1) contact someone at the company; 2) rest in own room; 3) visit hospital if necessary.
- If unsure how to visit a hospital, consult the registered support organisation for specified-skilled workers (登録支援機関) or the supervising support organisation described by the source for the relevant worker category.
- Consequences of working while unwell: own condition may worsen; illness may spread to co-workers/customers; negative impact on workplace health and operations.
- Morning preparation exercises at the morning meeting must be performed; knee bending/stretching exercises (屈伸運動) can help prevent back pain even when the group does not exercise together.
Add English and Sinhala steps and warnings. Preserve source terms and avoid inventing medical advice.
