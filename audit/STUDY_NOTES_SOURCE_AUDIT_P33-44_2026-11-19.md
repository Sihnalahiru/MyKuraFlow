## Page-by-page findings

### Printed p33 — Wing truck, Unic/crane truck and power gate
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX explains that the wing truck's sides and roof open like wings, making side loading/unloading and forklift pallet handling easier. It defines a Unic/crane truck as a flatbed fitted with a crane for lifting heavy cargo. It covers the rear-mounted power gate/tailgate lifter, raising and lowering cargo between ground and truck bed, alternative names, special education requirement, normal operation by the truck driver and prohibition on unauthorised operation.

### Printed p34 — Transport and warehouse terminology summary
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes all source terms in the summary table: container, chassis, head/tractor unit, devanning, forklift, power gate, wing, pallet and platform, with English and Sinhala definitions.

### Printed p35 — Warehouse storage terms and standard terms
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX distinguishes warehouse storage terms and refrigerated-warehouse storage terms, explains where each applies, states the notification duty under the Warehouse Business Act, and defines standard terms as terms whose content is determined by the Minister and applied after notification. Warehouse operator, depositor and the responsible Japanese minister are defined in English and Sinhala.

### Printed p36 — Main rules under standard warehouse terms
**Status: COVERED.**
All ten source topics are represented:
1. business hours and holidays notification;
2. charges for additional work;
3. cargo that may be refused;
4. storage application form;
5. contract formation on receipt and the two possible receipt/warehouse-entry documents;
6. external-only inspection at receipt, not contents inspection;
7. storage in the received condition/form using the warehouse's method;
8. storage period counted from warehouse entry date;
9. written document required for release;
10. circumstances where release may be refused.
English and Sinhala explanations are present.

### Printed p37 — Warehouse charges and storage fees
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX explains the two components of warehouse charges (storage fee and handling fee), notification to the Minister, storage-fee calculation as rate per unit multiplied by storage period, ad valorem and weight/volume rate components, choosing the larger of weight and volume calculations, and the three-period monthly system (days 1–10, 11–20 and 21–month-end).

### Printed p38 — Other storage rates and handling fees
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes per-item/per-pallet pricing, floor-area pricing (often monthly), refrigerated-warehouse rates varying by temperature, refrigerated storage's two-period system (days 1–15 and 16–month-end), handling-fee calculation by work-type rate multiplied by work quantity, receiving/release fees, and distribution-processing fees such as sorting and bagging.

### Printed p39 — Duty of care and storage management
**Status: SUBSTANTIALLY COVERED.**
The DOCX explains the duty of a prudent manager (善管注意義務), the four-step receipt-to-release flow, the meaning of hai-tsuke, all five storage checklist points, and regular internal/external building patrols with temperature/humidity, equipment and post-operation housekeeping checks.

**Review note:** Keep the Japanese technical term 善管注意義務 visible with the English/Sinhala explanation. The source's “greater care than one's own valuables” concept should not be weakened in translation.

### Printed p40 — Warehouse incidents and confidentiality
**Status: PARTIAL — incident classification table missing.**
The confidentiality section is covered: protect secrets and personal information learned at work; do not accept instructions from anyone other than the depositor; verify the identity of anyone asking about cargo; do not take photographs or videos inside the warehouse; and retain the two key worker attitudes (duty of care and confidentiality).

However, the source page also contains an eight-item incident/loss table, which is not reproduced in the corresponding DOCX page section:
- 火災 — fire in the warehouse;
- 盗難 — cargo theft;
- 濡損 — cargo becoming wet / water damage;
- 漏出 — leakage or contents escaping from cargo;
- 変質 — cargo condition/quality changing;
- 鼠害 — damage caused by rats/mice;
- 虫害 — damage caused by insects;
- 臭気（移り香） — odour transfer / cargo absorbing another cargo's smell.

**Required correction:** Add the complete eight-row bilingual incident table with accurate distinctions between wet damage and leakage, and between deterioration and odour transfer.

### Printed p41 — Quality management according to cargo characteristics
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX includes learning the cargo's characteristics and storage rules; checking temperature, humidity, leakage, change in condition, rodent/insect damage and odour transfer; reporting abnormalities immediately; measuring and submitting temperature/humidity records; and reporting swollen cartons, water droplets on racks and condensation on floors, ceilings and walls during hot/humid periods.

### Printed p42 — Odour-emitting and odour-emitting/absorbing cargo
**Status: COVERED.**
The DOCX distinguishes 発散貨物 (cargo that emits odour) from 放臭・吸気貨物 (cargo that emits its own odour and absorbs odours from other goods), gives the source examples for each, explains mixed/compound odour risk, and states the basic prevention rules. It also notes that cardboard, plastic and PET bottles can absorb odours.

### Printed p43 — Odour-control rules and efficient space use
**Status: PARTIAL — detailed odour-control decision table missing.**
The DOCX covers efficient use of space, keeping consolidated space available, vertical use of pallets/racks, authorised relocation (はい替え／倉替え), following the responsible person's instructions and reporting the new location.

But the source's three-situation odour-control table is not reproduced in the DOCX section:
1. Before cargo enters the warehouse: obtain a declaration from the depositor about the product's characteristics.
2. If warehouse odour may transfer to the cargo: discuss the product characteristics and agree on handling in advance.
3. If fragrance is a defining product characteristic: the depositor should improve packaging before entry, such as using double packaging, because preserving fragrance over a long storage period is difficult.

**Required correction:** Add all three situations, required actions and the source's note about double packaging and the difficulty of preserving fragrance over long periods.

### Printed p44 — 5S, warehouse organisation and reporting
**Status: PARTIAL — 5S definition table missing.**
The DOCX includes the warehouse organisation checklist (organise packaging/pallet materials and prevent shortages; keep tools in designated locations and ready for use; collect waste in designated areas) and regular inspection of long-staying cargo. It also covers reporting comments or concerns from drivers/customers and why small observations can prevent larger problems.

However, the source's five 5S definitions are not present in the DOCX section:
- 整理 (Seiri): separate necessary and unnecessary items;
- 整頓 (Seiton): put necessary items in their designated places;
- 清掃 (Seiso): clean/remove dirt;
- 清潔 (Seiketsu): maintain the clean condition continuously;
- しつけ (Shitsuke): follow established rules properly.

**Required correction:** Add the full 5S bilingual table, preserving the distinction between cleaning (清掃) and maintaining cleanliness (清潔).
